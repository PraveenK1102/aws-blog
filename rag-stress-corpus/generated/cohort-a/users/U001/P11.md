## Where things stand

I spent most of June on LangGraph, and it changed how I answer questions about the behavioural round. For Amazon, that round ended up rescheduled. Two rounds in, the pattern is clear: I over-prepare for Spring and under-prepare for follow-ups. I removed the tool list from my resume and replaced it with three decisions I could defend.

## How I prepared

A recruiter told me the first thirty seconds of a project explanation decide the rest of the conversation. Scheduling practice at the same hour each morning removed most of the friction of starting. The Amazon round came back as passed, which I had half expected. I rehearsed the Docker portion so heavily that the unscripted questions caught me flat.

Narrowing to Platform / DevOps roles felt like giving something up until the replies started improving.

## The round itself

There was a long gap before the Amazon reply, which I have learned not to read anything into. One question about LangGraph exposed that I had memorised the shape of an answer without the reasoning. I have started treating each rejection as a list of gaps instead of a verdict on the Platform / DevOps track. There is a difference between knowing LangGraph and defending a decision about LangGraph under time pressure.

## What I took from it

Half my notes from June are about communication rather than anything technical. Reading other people's interview write-ups helps, though their confidence is easy to mistake for my own readiness. I spent most of June on LangGraph, and it changed how I answer questions about my resume.
