## Where things stand

The part of my stack I lean on most is Docker. Preparing for Atlassian meant choosing between depth in Qdrant and breadth across Kubernetes; I chose depth. The follow-up thread at Atlassian was harder than the practice problems suggested, mainly because I had rehearsed the happy path. Something I keep getting wrong: I describe what I built before explaining why it needed building.

Practising with a timer changed my answers more than any amount of reading about Redis did.

## How I prepared

The prep that actually moved the needle was explaining one design decision without notes. I keep coming back to Kubernetes for this. The gap between July and now is mostly confidence rather than knowledge.

Two rounds in, the pattern is clear: I over-prepare for Redis and under-prepare for follow-ups.

## The round itself

I removed the tool list from my resume and replaced it with three decisions I could defend. My preparation now splits evenly between Docker fundamentals and talking through the project end to end.

## What I took from it

Did the Full Stack framing help? Slightly — recruiters asked better questions once I stopped hedging. Reading other people's interview write-ups helps, though their confidence is easy to mistake for my own readiness. Rejection stings less when it arrives with a specific reason attached, and this one did. Half my notes from July are about communication rather than anything technical.
