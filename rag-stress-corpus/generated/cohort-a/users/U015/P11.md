## The problem

When I finally measured it, latency was 49%. Two runs disagreed by enough that I stopped trusting the setup and rebuilt the measurement first. I keep a short list of results I do not believe yet, and revisit it weekly. The result held up in staging and quietly regressed the moment traffic patterns changed. Every improvement I could not reproduce twice turned out not to be an improvement. Because the harness came before the optimisation, I caught a regression that looked like a win.

## What I measured

Latency landed at 28% once I measured it end to end. Reproducibility mattered more here than cleverness, which is not how I would have ranked them in June. Keeping a log of every configuration felt tedious until I needed to explain a regression. Splitting the pipeline around the retrieval step made the failures much easier to localise. I spent an afternoon convinced the model was wrong before finding a bug in the loader.
## What I would change

The first version worked on the sample set and fell apart the moment real Kubernetes inputs arrived. Once I logged every configuration, explaining the regression to myself took minutes rather than a day. Batching improved throughput and made the tail latency considerably harder to reason about. Instrumenting the retrieval step cost a day and saved considerably more than that later.
