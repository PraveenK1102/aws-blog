## The problem

I now write the evaluation before the change, which has saved me from at least two false wins. Writing the failure cases down first made the design conversation much shorter. Running it end to end put accuracy at 19%. Because the harness came before the optimisation, I caught a regression that looked like a win. Two runs disagreed by enough that I stopped trusting the setup and rebuilt the measurement first. The result held up in staging and quietly regressed the moment traffic patterns changed.
## What I built

When I finally measured it, accuracy was 32%. I keep a short list of results I do not believe yet, and revisit it weekly. Two configurations differed by one parameter and by far more than one parameter's worth of output.

Instrumenting the chunking logic cost a day and saved considerably more than that later. Cleaning the PostgreSQL inputs produced a larger gain than any change to the model did. Reproducibility mattered more here than cleverness, which is not how I would have ranked them in July.
## What I measured

On the instrumented run, latency showed 5%. The tempting change was the model; the effective change was how documents were split. Comparing against a fixed baseline made the July numbers finally interpretable. Tuning Docker helped marginally; fixing how documents were split helped substantially. I rebuilt the chunking logic so the before-and-after comparison would actually mean something.
## What I would change

I spent an afternoon convinced the model was wrong before finding a bug in the loader. Was the gain from the model or from cleaning the PostgreSQL inputs? Mostly the inputs. The first version worked on the sample set and fell apart the moment real PostgreSQL inputs arrived. Caching Python lookups helped until invalidation became the more interesting problem.
