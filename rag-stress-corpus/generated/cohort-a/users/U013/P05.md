## The problem

A plausible answer citing the wrong passage is worse than an obvious failure, and much harder to catch. I keep a short list of results I do not believe yet, and revisit it weekly. Reproducibility mattered more here than cleverness, which is not how I would have ranked them in April.

The number I got for retrieval was 12%. The result held up in staging and quietly regressed the moment traffic patterns changed. I now write the evaluation before the change, which has saved me from at least two false wins.
## What I built

Accuracy landed at 5% once I measured it end to end. Caching Redis lookups helped until invalidation became the more interesting problem. Because the harness came before the optimisation, I caught a regression that looked like a win. In April I assumed the bottleneck was compute; it was almost entirely I/O.

I rebuilt the reranking pass so the before-and-after comparison would actually mean something. Comparing against a fixed baseline made the April numbers finally interpretable. Batching improved throughput and made the tail latency considerably harder to reason about.
## What I measured

My recorded figure for accuracy is 54%. Two configurations differed by one parameter and by far more than one parameter's worth of output. Every improvement I could not reproduce twice turned out not to be an improvement. The tempting change was the model; the effective change was how documents were split.
## What I would change

Two runs disagreed by enough that I stopped trusting the setup and rebuilt the measurement first. The fallback path had never been exercised, so nobody knew it returned an empty result. Latency turned out to be dominated by the evaluation harness, which I had assumed was cheap.
