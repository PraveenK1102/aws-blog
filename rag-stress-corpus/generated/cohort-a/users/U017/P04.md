## Getting there

People kept recommending Kadal Café, but I did not make it there this time — no visit. I had three places on the list and reached one of them properly. I nearly walked past the entrance twice; there is no signage worth mentioning. The afternoon heat decided the rest of the itinerary for us. The plan lasted about an hour before the side street sent us down a different street entirely.
## The place itself

Kadal Café came up more than once in conversation, though I never went in (idli). Prices and opening hours shift often enough around Chennai that I would check again before relying on them. Getting around Chennai on foot was slower and considerably better than the alternative. I had planned an itinerary and abandoned it within the hour, which was the right call. A short walk turned into a long one because the street kept being interesting. Arriving before seven meant Kadal Café was still calm and the staff had time to talk.
## What I ate

Walking back the long way through Chennai showed me a stretch of road I had completely missed. People kept recommending Kadal Café, but I did not make it there this time — dosa. Everyone recommends Kadal Café, and for once the recommendation held up.

By the time we reached the far end, the light had gone soft and the crowds had thinned. The weather turned halfway through and rearranged the afternoon completely.
## Notes for next time

We got to Chennai early enough that the courtyard was still quiet, which made the whole morning easier. The return journey was quieter and gave me time to write most of this. There was a queue by eight, so the early start justified itself. I asked at the counter rather than trusting the map, and got better directions. The Chennai traffic made the short route slower than the long one.
