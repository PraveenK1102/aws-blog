## Where things stand

My preparation now splits evenly between Kubernetes fundamentals and talking through the project end to end. My Google round result was passed. I spent most of June on Kubernetes, and it changed how I answer questions about the recruiter screen.

Keeping a list of the questions that made me pause has been more useful than any course. There was a long gap before the Google reply, which I have learned not to read anything into. Two rounds in, the pattern is clear: I over-prepare for FastAPI and under-prepare for follow-ups.
## How I prepared

When they asked why vector search and not something simpler, I did not have a crisp answer ready. Something I keep getting wrong: I describe what I built before explaining why it needed building. The Google round came back as rescheduled, which I had half expected. Scheduling practice at the same hour each morning removed most of the friction of starting. I have started treating each rejection as a list of gaps instead of a verdict on the Generative AI Engineer track.
## The round itself

The prep that actually moved the needle was explaining one design decision without notes. Reading other people's interview write-ups helps, though their confidence is easy to mistake for my own readiness. I stopped applying broadly in June; the reply rate went up almost immediately.

## What I took from it

Preparing for Google meant choosing between depth in Kubernetes and breadth across vector search; I chose depth. Did the Generative AI Engineer framing help? Slightly — recruiters asked better questions once I stopped hedging. Half my notes from June are about communication rather than anything technical.
