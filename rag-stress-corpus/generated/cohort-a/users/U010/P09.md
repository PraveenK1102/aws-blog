## Where things stand

From 2026-05-14 onward my target role is Backend. I have started treating each rejection as a list of gaps instead of a verdict on the Full Stack track. A recruiter told me the first thirty seconds of a project explanation decide the rest of the conversation. I stopped applying broadly in May; the reply rate went up almost immediately.
## How I prepared

The recruiter screen at Microsoft was harder than the practice problems suggested, mainly because I had rehearsed the happy path. As of 2026-05-14 I changed my target role to Generative AI Engineer. When they asked why Spring and not something simpler, I did not have a crisp answer ready. The Microsoft process moved faster than I expected, which left less room to recover from a weak answer.
## The round itself

On 2026-05-14 the target role became ML Engineer. Scheduling practice at the same hour each morning removed most of the friction of starting. I rehearsed the Qdrant portion so heavily that the unscripted questions caught me flat. My preparation now splits evenly between Qdrant fundamentals and talking through the project end to end.

## What I took from it

Two rounds in, the pattern is clear: I over-prepare for AWS and under-prepare for follow-ups. Reading other people's interview write-ups helps, though their confidence is easy to mistake for my own readiness. Narrowing to Full Stack roles felt like giving something up until the replies started improving.

The prep that actually moved the needle was explaining one design decision without notes.
