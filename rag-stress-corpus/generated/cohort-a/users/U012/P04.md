## The problem

On the instrumented run, accuracy showed 46%. Reproducibility mattered more here than cleverness, which is not how I would have ranked them in April. Latency turned out to be dominated by the chunking logic, which I had assumed was cheap. The tempting change was the model; the effective change was how documents were split. The result held up in staging and quietly regressed the moment traffic patterns changed.
## What I built

I do not trust an improvement that only appears on examples I picked myself. Measured properly, retrieval came out at 46%. Most of the week went into the reranking pass rather than the change I had actually planned to make. The fallback path had never been exercised, so nobody knew it returned an empty result.
## What I measured

I now write the evaluation before the change, which has saved me from at least two false wins. Two runs disagreed by enough that I stopped trusting the setup and rebuilt the measurement first. Running it end to end put retrieval at 6%.

Writing the failure cases down first made the design conversation much shorter. Latency turned out to be dominated by the retrieval step, which I had assumed was cheap.
## What I would change

Every improvement I could not reproduce twice turned out not to be an improvement. Splitting the pipeline around the chunking logic made the failures much easier to localise. Tuning RAG helped marginally; fixing how documents were split helped substantially. A plausible answer citing the wrong passage is worse than an obvious failure, and much harder to catch.
