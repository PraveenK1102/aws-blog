## Where things stand

For Adobe, that round ended up failed. Did the Data Engineer framing help? Slightly — recruiters asked better questions once I stopped hedging. Reading other people's interview write-ups helps, though their confidence is easy to mistake for my own readiness. Practising with a timer changed my answers more than any amount of reading about AWS did.
## How I prepared

My Adobe round result was passed. When they asked why Redis and not something simpler, I did not have a crisp answer ready. Half my notes from April are about communication rather than anything technical.

Two rounds in, the pattern is clear: I over-prepare for Kubernetes and under-prepare for follow-ups. I spent most of April on Kubernetes, and it changed how I answer questions about the take-home.

## The round itself

Narrowing to Data Engineer roles felt like giving something up until the replies started improving. The strongest answer I gave all week was the one where I admitted a trade-off I had got wrong. The Adobe process moved faster than I expected, which left less room to recover from a weak answer.

I have started treating each rejection as a list of gaps instead of a verdict on the Data Engineer track.

## What I took from it

Something I keep getting wrong: I describe what I built before explaining why it needed building. After the recruiter screen, I wrote down the two moments where my answer thinned out rather than rewriting everything. Looking back at April, the useful hours were the ones I spent explaining vector search out loud.
