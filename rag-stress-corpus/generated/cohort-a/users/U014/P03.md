## The problem

I do not trust an improvement that only appears on examples I picked myself. Once I logged every configuration, explaining the regression to myself took minutes rather than a day. Was the gain from the model or from cleaning the AWS inputs? Mostly the inputs.

Latency landed at 10% once I measured it end to end. I rebuilt the scoring function so the before-and-after comparison would actually mean something. The result held up in staging and quietly regressed the moment traffic patterns changed.
## What I built

Comparing against a fixed baseline made the March numbers finally interpretable. The fallback path had never been exercised, so nobody knew it returned an empty result. Instrumenting the scoring function cost a day and saved considerably more than that later. The number I got for accuracy was 15%. I spent an afternoon convinced the model was wrong before finding a bug in the loader. The first version worked on the sample set and fell apart the moment real vector search inputs arrived.
## What I measured

Measured properly, latency came out at 41%. Cleaning the Redis inputs produced a larger gain than any change to the model did. I now write the evaluation before the change, which has saved me from at least two false wins. The tempting change was the model; the effective change was how documents were split. The interesting failures were the ones where the output read well but cited the wrong source.
## What I would change

Because the harness came before the optimisation, I caught a regression that looked like a win. I keep a short list of results I do not believe yet, and revisit it weekly. Splitting the pipeline around the ingestion path made the failures much easier to localise. Two configurations differed by one parameter and by far more than one parameter's worth of output.
